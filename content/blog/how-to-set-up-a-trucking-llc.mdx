To set up a trucking LLC, you choose a business name and state, file formation papers (usually called articles of organization) with that state, get an EIN from the IRS, put an operating agreement in place, open a business bank account, and then move on to FMCSA registration, insurance, and your BOC-3. An LLC can separate your business and personal finances, but it doesn't replace insurance, and a CPA or attorney should confirm what fits your situation.

## Do I need an LLC to start a trucking company?

You don't have to form an LLC to register as a motor carrier, but many owner-operators do because it can put a legal wall between business debts and personal assets. It can also make your business look more established to brokers, insurers, and lenders. Which structure is best for taxes and liability depends on your situation, so ask a CPA or attorney, especially before you sign a truck loan.

## What are the steps to set up a trucking LLC?

1. **Choose your business name.** Check that it's available in your state and not confusingly close to another carrier. Pick something you're happy to put on your truck and paperwork.
2. **Pick your state.** Most owner-operators form the LLC in the state where they live and operate. State fees and annual requirements vary, so look them up for yours.
3. **File the formation papers.** This is typically articles of organization filed with your state's business filing office. You'll also name a registered agent.
4. **Write an operating agreement.** Even for a single-owner LLC, it documents how the business runs and supports the separation between you and the company.
5. **Get an EIN.** This is your business's federal tax ID from the IRS, and you'll need it for banking, insurance, and FMCSA registration.
6. **Open a business bank account.** Keep business and personal money separate from day one. That habit protects the liability separation an LLC is meant to give you.
7. **Register with FMCSA.** Apply for your USDOT number and MC authority. See [how to get your MC authority](/resources/how-to-get-mc-authority).
8. **Finish the filings.** Arrange insurance, file your [BOC-3](/resources/boc-3-filing), and work through the [DOT compliance checklist](/resources/dot-compliance-checklist-new-authority).

## Should the name match everywhere?

Yes. Use your exact legal business name consistently across your state filing, EIN, bank account, FMCSA registration, insurance, and carrier packets. Small mismatches can slow onboarding with brokers and cause problems with filings.

## What are common mistakes?

- Mixing personal and business money, which weakens the separation
- Forgetting your state's annual report or fee, which can put the LLC out of good standing
- Skipping the operating agreement
- Assuming an LLC replaces insurance. It doesn't.
- Not telling insurers and brokers the legal name you registered
