To get your MC authority, you register your trucking business with the FMCSA to obtain a USDOT number and MC (motor carrier) number, secure the required insurance, file a BOC-3 process agent form, and complete your carrier setup. Once your authority is active and insurance is filed, you can start booking your own loads. The process usually takes a few weeks, mostly waiting on the FMCSA's mandatory review period.

## The basic steps to your own authority

**1. Form your business.** Set up your business entity (many owner-operators use an LLC) and get an EIN from the IRS.

**2. Register with the FMCSA.** Apply for your USDOT number and operating authority (MC number) through the FMCSA. This is the core registration that lets you operate as a carrier.

**3. Get the required insurance.** You'll need the liability and cargo coverage the FMCSA requires. Your insurance company files proof with the FMCSA, which is part of activating your authority.

**4. File your BOC-3.** This designates a process agent in each state — a required filing, usually handled quickly through a service.

**5. Wait out the FMCSA review period.** After you apply, there's a mandatory waiting period before your authority becomes active. This is often the longest part.

**6. Set up to haul.** Once active, set up your carrier packets with brokers, arrange factoring if you want faster payment, and line up your first loads.

> This is a general overview, not legal or compliance advice. Requirements change and vary by situation — always confirm current rules directly with the FMCSA.

## The hardest part for new authorities: getting loaded

Getting the authority is paperwork. The real challenge starts after: a brand-new MC has no broker relationships and no track record, and many dispatchers and brokers are cautious with new authorities. This is where a lot of new owner-operators struggle in their first 90 days.
