Owner-operator insurance costs vary widely, because the price depends on your authority's age, driving record, equipment, routes, cargo, and coverage limits, so there's no single number worth quoting. What's fixed is the federal minimum: most for-hire carriers hauling non-hazardous freight in heavy trucks must carry at least $750,000 in public liability coverage, and your insurer must file proof with FMCSA before your authority can become active.

## What insurance is required for an owner-operator?

For for-hire carriers hauling general freight in vehicles of 10,001 pounds or more, FMCSA requires a minimum of $750,000 in public liability coverage. Certain hazardous materials carry much higher minimums. Your insurance company files proof of coverage with FMCSA directly (a BMC-91 or BMC-91X filing), and an MCS-90 endorsement is part of that coverage. You can't submit that proof yourself, and FMCSA won't activate your authority until the required coverage is on file.

Federal rules don't require cargo insurance for general freight, but many brokers and shippers do, so most carriers have it.

## What other coverage should I consider?

Depending on how you operate, owner-operators commonly look at:

- **Cargo coverage** for the freight you haul
- **Physical damage** for your tractor and trailer
- **Non-trucking (bobtail) liability** when you're driving without a load
- **Occupational accident** coverage, since many owner-operators aren't covered by workers' compensation
- **General liability**, which some customers ask for

## What affects what I pay?

- **How long you've had authority.** New authorities often pay more because there's no history to rate.
- **Your driving and claims record.**
- **Your equipment:** age, value, and type.
- **Where and how you run:** radius, states, and garaging location.
- **What you haul:** cargo type and value.
- **Limits and deductibles:** higher limits raise the premium, higher deductibles can lower it.
- **How you pay:** payment plans and down payments vary.

## Why is insurance harder for new authorities?

Insurers price risk from history, and a brand-new authority has none. That often means fewer carriers willing to quote and higher premiums in the first year. A clean record, steady operations, and sound safety practices help you earn better terms at renewal. ## How do I get better quotes?

- Work with an agent who specializes in trucking, and get several quotes.
- Compare limits, deductibles, and exclusions, not just the monthly price.
- Ask what could cause a cancellation. A lapsed filing can put your authority at risk.
- Keep your driving record and paperwork clean before you shop.
