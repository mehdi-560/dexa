The MCS-150 biennial update is a form every motor carrier with a USDOT number must file every 24 months, even if nothing about the business has changed. Your deadline comes from your USDOT number: the last digit sets the month, and the next-to-last digit sets whether you file in odd or even years. FMCSA says a missed update leads to deactivation of your USDOT number and may bring civil penalties of up to $1,000 per day, capped at $10,000.

## What is the MCS-150?

The MCS-150 is FMCSA's Motor Carrier Identification Report. It's the form that keeps your USDOT record accurate: legal name, address, type of operation, number of trucks and drivers, and mileage. The biennial update is the same information refreshed every two years so FMCSA's records match reality.

## How do I find my MCS-150 deadline?

Look at the last two digits of your USDOT number:

- **The last digit sets the month.** It runs 1 = January, 2 = February, and so on through 9 = September, with 0 = October. You must file by the last day of that month.
- **The next-to-last digit sets the year.** If it's odd, you file in odd-numbered years. If it's even, you file in even-numbered years.

For example, a USDOT number ending in 36 has a last digit of 6 (June) and a next-to-last digit of 3 (odd), so the update is due by the end of June in every odd-numbered year.

Some websites print different schedule tables, so check yours against FMCSA's own "Updating Your Registration" page.

## How do I file the biennial update?

FMCSA says updating your information is free. Its page recommends filing online through the Ask FMCSA website, and warns that mailing a paper form can delay processing. If you mail it, FMCSA must receive it by your due date. FMCSA is also modernizing its registration systems, so confirm the current filing steps on FMCSA's site before you start. Be careful with sites that charge fees to do it for you, since the filing itself costs nothing.

## What happens if I miss the deadline?

According to FMCSA, failing to complete a biennial update results in deactivation of your USDOT number and may result in civil penalties of up to $1,000 per day, not to exceed $10,000. A deactivated number can stop you from operating legally and can hold up everything else, including getting onboarded with brokers who check your registration. Civil penalty amounts are adjusted over time, so confirm current figures with FMCSA.

## Do I have to update only every two years?

No. Beyond the biennial schedule, FMCSA asks you to update your record in a timely manner whenever details change, such as your legal business name, address, or other registration information. You also have to file the biennial update even if you stopped running or the business has changed.

## How do I avoid missing it?

- Put your due month on a calendar with a reminder 60 days early
- Keep your USDOT PIN and login information somewhere safe
- Check your status on FMCSA's SAFER Company Snapshot after you file
