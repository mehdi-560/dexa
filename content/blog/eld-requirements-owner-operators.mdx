Most interstate truck drivers who must keep records of duty status (RODS) are required to use a registered electronic logging device (ELD) instead of paper logs. The main exemptions are drivers who need RODS on 8 or fewer days in any 30-day period, driveaway-towaway operations, vehicles with engines built before model year 2000, and short-haul drivers who meet the short-haul exception. Being ELD-exempt does not mean being exempt from hours-of-service rules.

## Do owner-operators need an ELD?

Yes, in most cases. If you're required to keep records of duty status under FMCSA's hours-of-service rules, you generally have to record them on an ELD that is on FMCSA's list of registered devices. Being an owner-operator with a single truck does not exempt you. The rule follows the type of driving you do, not the size of your company.

## Who is exempt from the ELD requirement?

The commonly cited exemptions are:

- **8-in-30:** drivers who are required to keep RODS on no more than 8 days in any 30-day period can use paper logs on those days.
- **Driveaway-towaway:** drivers in driveaway-towaway operations, where the vehicle being moved is the cargo.
- **Older engines:** vehicles with engines manufactured before model year 2000.
- **Short-haul:** drivers who qualify for the short-haul exception and stay within its distance and time limits.

Check the details and any recent changes on FMCSA's website before you rely on one of these. Sources differ on the finer points, especially for short-haul.

## Does my truck's age matter?

For the older-engine exemption, what counts is the model year of the **engine**, not the truck or chassis. A newer truck with an older engine may still qualify, while an older truck with a newer engine swap may not. Check the engine's model year on its label or documentation, and keep proof in case an inspector asks.

## What does "ELD-exempt" actually mean?

It means you don't have to use an ELD. You still must follow hours-of-service rules, keep paper logs or the required time records, and follow all other regulations. Many drivers confuse the two, and that's a common source of violations.

## How do I choose an ELD?

- Confirm the device is on FMCSA's registered ELD list. Not every product sold as an ELD qualifies.
- Compare total cost, including hardware, monthly fees, and contract terms. Avoid being locked into a long term you can't exit.
- Check the support: what happens if it malfunctions on the road?
- Make sure you can show logs at inspection and transfer data the way an officer requires.
- Look for helpful extras, like mileage by state that can make [IFTA reporting](/resources/ifta-reporting-for-owner-operators) easier.

## What happens if I don't have a required ELD?

Operating without a required ELD can lead to violations, fines, and being placed out of service until the problem is fixed, which means a delayed load and lost money. It also tends to show up in your safety record. Getting your logging setup right early saves time later.
