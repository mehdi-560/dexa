The key difference is who they work for. A **truck dispatcher works for the carrier** — finding loads, negotiating rates, and handling paperwork on the carrier's behalf. A **freight broker works between the shipper and the carrier** — they represent the shipper's freight and connect it to a truck. A dispatcher is on your side of the table; a broker is on the other.

## What a freight broker does

A freight broker is the middleman between a company that needs freight moved (the shipper) and the carrier that moves it. Brokers:

- Represent the **shipper's** freight
- Post loads and find carriers to haul them
- Earn a margin between what the shipper pays and what the carrier is paid
- Are licensed by the FMCSA and must carry a surety bond

## What a truck dispatcher does

A dispatcher represents the **carrier**. They:

- Find and book loads that fit the carrier's truck and lanes
- Negotiate with brokers to get the carrier the best rate
- Handle the carrier's paperwork and broker setups
- Are paid by the carrier, usually a percentage of each load

## Why the difference matters to your paycheck

Because a broker earns a margin on the load, their incentive is to pay the carrier *less*. A dispatcher's incentive is the opposite — since they're paid a percentage of what you earn, they want to negotiate the rate *up*. When you work with a dispatcher, you have someone whose interests match yours sitting across from the broker.

## Can one company be both?

Some companies hold both broker and dispatch authority, but the roles are distinct and it's worth knowing which hat they're wearing on a given load. A dedicated dispatcher who only represents carriers has no conflict of interest — they win only when you win.
