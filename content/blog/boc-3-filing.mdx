A BOC-3 is the FMCSA form that designates a legal process agent in each state where a carrier operates, someone who can accept court papers on the carrier's behalf. For-hire carriers applying for operating authority must have a BOC-3 on file before FMCSA will activate that authority. You don't file it yourself: a process agent company files it with FMCSA electronically for a fee.

## What is a BOC-3?

BOC-3 stands for "Designation of Agents for Service of Process." If someone needs to serve you with legal papers in a state where you have no office, the process agent receives them and forwards them to you. It's FMCSA's way of making sure carriers can be legally reached in every state they run through, even if the carrier is based a thousand miles away.

## Who needs a BOC-3?

Carriers applying for for-hire operating authority (an MC number) need one on file, along with the other required filings. Freight brokers and freight forwarders also need process agents. FMCSA won't grant operating authority until the BOC-3 is on file, alongside your insurance filing.

## What does a process agent actually do?

A process agent accepts legal documents, like lawsuit papers, on your behalf and sends them to you. Most process agent companies cover every state with one blanket designation, which is what most carriers use. That way you don't have to name a separate person in each state.

## How do I file a BOC-3?

1. Choose a process agent company that offers blanket coverage for all states.
2. Give them your company name and USDOT or MC number.
3. They file the designation electronically with FMCSA.
4. Keep your confirmation, then check that FMCSA shows it on file.

Process agent companies charge fees that vary by provider, so compare them. Make sure the one you pick actually covers every state.

## What happens if my BOC-3 is missing or lapses?

Without a BOC-3 on file, your authority can't be activated. If a filing is later cancelled or the process agent company stops covering you, your authority can be put at risk, so keep your information current and read any notices from your provider.

## How is the BOC-3 different from other filings?

- **BOC-3:** your process agents, filed by a process agent company
- **BMC-91 / BMC-91X:** your insurance proof, filed by your insurer
- **MCS-150:** your carrier registration information, kept current by you (see [the MCS-150 update guide](/resources/mcs-150-biennial-update))
- **UCR:** an annual registration for interstate carriers, separate from the others
