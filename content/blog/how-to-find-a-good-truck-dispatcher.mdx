To find a good truck dispatcher, look for five things: transparent percentage pricing with no hidden fees, no long-term contract, real reviews from other carriers, strong broker relationships, and a focus on your net-per-mile rather than just a low fee. The best dispatcher isn't the cheapest — it's the one who puts the most money in your pocket after their cut.

## What to look for in a dispatcher

**Transparent pricing.** A good dispatcher states one clear rate — usually a percentage of the load — with no setup fees or surprise add-ons. If the pricing is vague or keeps changing, that's a red flag.

**No long-term contract.** The best dispatchers keep your business by performing every week, not by locking you into paperwork. Look for month-to-month or no-contract terms.

**Real reviews and references.** Look for feedback from actual owner-operators. Carriers talk — a dispatcher with a good reputation in the community is worth more than one with a slick website and no track record.

**Strong broker relationships.** Established broker connections mean better loads and faster booking. This is especially valuable for new authorities who haven't built those relationships yet.

**Focus on net-per-mile, not just the fee.** A dispatcher charging 5% who books cheap freight can leave you with less than one charging 8% who negotiates hard. Ask how they think about your take-home, not just their percentage.

## Questions to ask before you sign up

- What's your fee, and are there any other charges?
- Is there a contract, or can I stop anytime?
- What equipment and lanes do you specialize in?
- How do you handle detention and problem loads?
- Can I talk to a carrier you currently dispatch?

## Red flags to avoid

Be cautious of dispatchers who demand long contracts, can't explain their pricing simply, have no verifiable reviews, or pressure you to sign immediately. Legitimate dispatchers are happy to answer questions and let their track record speak.
