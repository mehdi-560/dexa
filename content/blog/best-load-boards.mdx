The best load boards for owner-operators are the large paid boards like DAT and Truckstop.com, which have the most freight and the best rate data, along with a handful of free and niche boards useful for new authorities on a tight budget. The right board depends on your equipment, lanes, and whether you're paying for premium rate tools — but the biggest boards give you the most loads to choose from and the leverage to negotiate.

## What a load board does

A load board is a marketplace where brokers and shippers post available freight and carriers find loads to haul. You search by equipment, origin, and destination, then contact the broker to book. The board itself doesn't set your rate — it shows you what's available so you can negotiate.

## The major paid load boards

The largest boards have the most freight, which matters because more options mean more leverage to hold out for a better rate. They also include rate analytics — data on what lanes are actually paying — which helps you avoid underpriced loads. For a full-time owner-operator, a paid board usually pays for itself through the better loads it surfaces.

## Free load boards for new authorities

If you're a brand-new authority watching every dollar, there are free and lower-cost boards to get started. They have less freight and fewer tools, but they're a way to begin before committing to a premium subscription. Many carriers start free and upgrade once they're running steadily.

## How to actually use a load board well

- **Know your cost per mile first** so you never book below your break-even. Use our [cost-per-mile calculator](/tools/cost-per-mile).
- **Factor in deadhead** — a high rate with 150 empty miles to reach it may pay less than a modest rate nearby. (More on [reducing deadhead](/resources/what-is-deadhead-reduce-empty-miles).)
- **Check the rate data** before you call, so you negotiate from knowledge.
- **Vet the broker** before hauling — see [how to avoid double brokering](/resources/how-to-avoid-double-brokering).
