Deadhead is the distance a truck travels empty — with no load on board — usually driving to a pickup after dropping off the last delivery. Those empty miles burn fuel and time but earn nothing, so they directly lower your take-home. Reducing deadhead is one of the most effective ways to raise your real earnings per mile without booking a single higher-paying load.

## Why deadhead matters so much

Every mile costs you money — fuel, wear, and time — whether or not you're loaded. So deadhead miles are pure expense. If you drive 100 empty miles to reach a load, the cost of those miles has to come out of what that load pays. A load that looks great on paper can turn mediocre once you account for the empty miles to get to it.

This is why your **effective rate per mile** (revenue divided by *all* miles, loaded and empty) matters more than the posted rate. Cutting deadhead raises that number for free.

## How to reduce deadhead miles

- **Book your next load before you deliver** so you're not stranded empty in a dead market.
- **Plan loads that end near freight** — delivering into an area with strong outbound freight means a shorter empty run to your next pickup.
- **Know the strong and weak markets** — some regions have plenty of outbound freight; others leave you deadheading out. (See [best states for owner-operators](/resources/best-states-for-owner-operators).)
- **Weigh deadhead into every rate decision** — a nearby modest load can beat a distant high-paying one once empty miles are counted.

## What's a normal amount of deadhead?

Some deadhead is unavoidable — freight rarely lines up perfectly end to end. The goal isn't zero; it's keeping empty miles as low as practical so they don't eat your margin. Consistently high deadhead is a sign of reactive booking, not planned routing.

## How a dispatcher cuts your deadhead

Good dispatching is largely about sequencing loads so each delivery sets up the next pickup with minimal empty running. A dispatcher watching the whole board and planning several loads ahead can keep your truck loaded far more efficiently than booking one load at a time.
