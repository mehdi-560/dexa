Switching dispatch companies is straightforward if you do it in the right order: finish your current loads, have your next dispatcher set up before you leave, keep copies of your carrier packets, and check your contract for any notice period. The biggest mistake carriers make is quitting their current dispatcher before the new one is ready, leaving a gap where no one is booking loads.

## When switching makes sense

There are legitimate reasons to switch and there are frustrations that might be solved by a conversation instead. A switch makes sense when:

- Your net-per-mile has been consistently lower than you think it should be
- Loads are being booked without proper rate negotiation
- Communication has broken down and problems aren't getting resolved
- You're being locked in to loads or lanes that don't work for your business
- The fee structure changed, or you found a better fit for your equipment and lanes

A switch might not be necessary if the issue is a rough patch, a seasonal market, or a miscommunication that could be fixed directly. Talk to your dispatcher first. If the problem persists, then plan your exit.

## Check your contract first

Before anything else, read whatever agreement you signed. Some dispatchers require advance notice, so check how much. Some have clauses about the loads you're currently running. Understand what you agreed to so you can exit cleanly without disputes.

If there's no contract (which is common with good dispatchers who operate month-to-month), you typically have more flexibility. But notify your current dispatcher professionally regardless — the trucking industry is a small community.

## The right sequence for switching

**1. Find your next dispatcher first.** Talk to candidates, ask the right questions (see [how to find a good truck dispatcher](/resources/how-to-find-a-good-truck-dispatcher)), and have your new dispatcher ready to onboard you before you give notice. Don't create a gap.

**2. Finish your current loads.** Don't walk away from a load mid-haul. Complete what's booked, deliver, and settle up any open rate confirmations or accessorials.

**3. Give notice properly.** Tell your current dispatcher you're moving on. Keep it professional. You may need them to send final paperwork or release broker packets.

**4. Collect your carrier packets.** Your carrier packets with brokers belong to your company, not your dispatcher. Make sure you have the information for every broker your current dispatcher set you up with. These are yours.

**5. Start with the new dispatcher.** Have your authority documents, insurance certificate, and a completed W-9 ready. A good dispatcher will tell you exactly what they need to get you onboarded quickly.

## Will I lose loads during the switch?

If you plan it right, no. The gap risk comes from leaving before the new dispatcher is ready. If you have a new service lined up and your paperwork is in order, downtime should be short while the new dispatcher gets your packets set up. Ask how long onboarding takes before you leave your current service.

## What to tell your new dispatcher upfront

Give them an honest picture of your lanes, your preferred pickup radius, your truck, and any equipment limitations. Tell them what didn't work with the previous service. The more context they have, the faster they can book you well.
