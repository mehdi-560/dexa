The best truck dispatch company is the one that raises your net-per-mile after its fee. It is not the one with the lowest percentage or the loudest marketing. The right choice depends on your equipment, your lanes, how old your authority is, and how much support you want. Below is how to compare dispatch services fairly, what they commonly charge, and a look at some well-known options. Details were last checked in October 2026 and can change, so confirm everything directly with any company you consider.

**Disclosure:** Dexent Logistics publishes this page and is listed in it. We've limited what we say about other companies to what they publish themselves or what is widely reported, and we point you to the questions that matter instead of ranking anyone. Read independent reviews and talk to more than one dispatcher before you decide.

## How do you judge a dispatch company?

Compare every company on the same seven points:

- **Net-per-mile after the fee.** A low percentage on cheap loads can leave you with less than a higher percentage on well-negotiated loads.
- **A clear total fee.** Ask for every charge in writing: the percentage or weekly rate, any monthly fee, and any add-ons.
- **Contract terms.** Month-to-month or no-contract terms mean the dispatcher has to keep earning your business.
- **Equipment and lane fit.** A service strong on dry van may be thinner on flatbed. Ask about your equipment and your regions.
- **Broker vetting.** Ask how they check brokers before booking. See [how to avoid double brokering](/resources/how-to-avoid-double-brokering).
- **Who you deal with.** A dedicated dispatcher who learns your preferences is different from a rotating desk.
- **References.** Ask to speak with a current carrier who runs your type of equipment.

## How much do truck dispatch companies charge?

Most services use one of two models. Many charge a percentage of each load's gross rate, and third-party and competitor comparison pages commonly report a range of roughly 5% to 10%, with a few advertising less. Others charge a flat weekly fee per truck. Hybrid structures exist too, such as a lower percentage plus a monthly fee.

Neither model is automatically better. Here is the same hypothetical week, with $5,000 in gross loads, under three structures:

- 8% of gross: $400
- 6% of gross: $300
- $250 flat weekly: $250

On fees alone the flat rate wins at that volume. But if a dispatcher who charges more books loads that pay meaningfully more per mile, they can still leave you ahead. Run the numbers at your own weekly gross, including slow weeks. For the full breakdown, see [percentage vs. flat fee dispatch](/resources/dispatch-percentage-vs-flat-fee) and [how dispatchers get paid](/resources/how-do-dispatchers-get-paid).

## What are the red flags when choosing a dispatcher?

- Pricing that only becomes clear after you sign
- Long contracts with penalties for leaving
- Pressure to commit immediately
- No clear answer on how they vet brokers
- No clear answer on how they handle [detention](/resources/detention-pay-trucking)
- No willingness to connect you with a current carrier

## Which dispatch service is best for new authorities?

Look for a dispatcher that explicitly works with brand-new MC authorities, since some services require a minimum operating history. Ask whether new carriers pay a different rate or a monthly fee during the first months, and what happens after. For the full picture, see [do new owner-operators need a dispatcher](/resources/do-new-owner-operators-need-a-dispatcher) and [how to find a good truck dispatcher](/resources/how-to-find-a-good-truck-dispatcher).

## What is the honest bottom line?

No dispatch company is right for every carrier. Work out your own break-even with the [cost-per-mile calculator](/tools/cost-per-mile), ask each company the same questions, and compare what you would actually keep per week. That number, not the advertised percentage, tells you whether the fee is worth it.
