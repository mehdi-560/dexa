Owner-operators often gross somewhere between $150,000 and $300,000+ per year, but the number that matters is take-home after expenses — and that's much lower, since fuel, truck payments, insurance, maintenance, and fees eat a large share of the gross. What you actually keep depends far more on your cost control and the rates you run than on miles alone.

## Gross vs. net — the number that matters

Gross revenue is what the truck brings in before expenses. It's the big, impressive number — but it's not your pay. **Net income** is what's left after all the costs of running the truck, and that's what you actually live on. Two owner-operators grossing the same amount can take home wildly different pay depending on their expenses and the rates they book.

## Where the money goes

Typical major expenses for an owner-operator include:

- **Fuel** — usually the single largest cost
- **Truck payment** — if financing the tractor
- **Insurance** — often significant, especially for new authorities
- **Maintenance and tires** — set money aside; breakdowns are expensive
- **Dispatch, factoring, and permits** — operating costs
- **Taxes** — set aside a portion of every settlement

After all of this, take-home is a fraction of the gross. This is exactly why your **cost per mile** and the **rates you run** matter so much.

## The lever most owner-operators underuse: rate quality

You can only cut costs so far — fuel and insurance are what they are. The bigger lever is the **rate on each load**. Running better-paying freight and cutting deadhead (empty) miles raises your net without raising your costs. A good dispatcher earns their fee here: booking higher-rate loads and keeping your truck loaded.

## How to increase your take-home

- Know your cost per mile and never book blindly below it
- Cut deadhead miles — empty miles cost you money
- Run better-paying lanes and equipment where you can
- Negotiate every load, or use a dispatcher who does
