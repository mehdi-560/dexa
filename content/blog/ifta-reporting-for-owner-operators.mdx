IFTA, the International Fuel Tax Agreement, lets a carrier that runs across state lines file one quarterly fuel tax return with its home (base) state instead of filing with every state it drives through. If your truck is a "qualified motor vehicle" and travels in two or more member jurisdictions, you need an IFTA license and decals, and your return is due four times a year: April 30, July 31, October 31, and January 31.

## What is IFTA and why does it exist?

Fuel tax is owed to the state where you burn the fuel, not necessarily the state where you buy it. IFTA turns that into one filing. You report the miles you drove and the fuel you bought in each jurisdiction, and your base state works out what you owe or are owed and settles up with the other states for you.

## Do I need an IFTA license?

You generally need one if your truck is a qualified motor vehicle and operates in two or more IFTA member jurisdictions (member states and Canadian provinces). A qualified motor vehicle is one used to haul people or property that has:

- two axles and a gross or registered gross vehicle weight over 26,000 pounds, or
- three or more axles, regardless of weight, or
- a combination (truck plus trailer) with a combined weight over 26,000 pounds.

Recreational vehicles don't count. You apply through your base state, which issues one IFTA license plus two decals per qualified vehicle. The decals go on both sides of the cab, and the license is kept in the truck.

## When are IFTA returns due?

Returns are due on the last day of the month after each quarter ends:

- **Q1 (January–March):** April 30
- **Q2 (April–June):** July 31
- **Q3 (July–September):** October 31
- **Q4 (October–December):** January 31

If a due date lands on a weekend or holiday, it generally moves to the next business day.

## What records do I need for IFTA?

Auditors look for two sets of records that agree with each other:

- **Distance:** miles driven in each jurisdiction, with trip dates, origin, destination, and the route. Many carriers pull this from their ELD or a mileage-tracking app.
- **Fuel:** every fuel purchase, with the date, gallons, price, seller, and the state where you bought it, backed by receipts.

Plan to keep these records for at least four years, and confirm the exact rule with your base state.

## Do I file IFTA if my truck didn't run?

Yes. File a return every quarter even if you drove no miles or bought no fuel. Missing a filing can bring penalties and interest, and some states can suspend or revoke an IFTA license for non-filing.

## What are the most common IFTA mistakes?

- Skipping a quarter because "I don't owe anything"
- Missing or misplaced decals, which can mean a citation at inspection
- Rebuilding miles from memory instead of trip records
- Confusing IFTA (fuel tax) with IRP (apportioned registration plates), which is a separate program

## How does IFTA affect my profit?

IFTA isn't an extra tax on top of what you already pay. The tax is built into pump prices, and your return reconciles what you paid with what you owe based on where you actually drove. Depending on your routes, you may owe a little or get a credit. Either way, accurate records protect you in an audit, and your fuel and compliance costs belong in your [cost per mile](/tools/cost-per-mile).
