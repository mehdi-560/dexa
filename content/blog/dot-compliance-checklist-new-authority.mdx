A new motor carrier needs to line up six things before the first load: USDOT number and MC authority, a BOC-3 and insurance filings, a drug and alcohol testing program, hours-of-service compliance (usually an ELD), a driver qualification file, and the right tax and registration filings for your truck. After you start running, FMCSA monitors new carriers in its New Entrant program for 18 months, including a safety audit.

## What registrations and filings do I need?

- **USDOT number and MC (operating) authority** from FMCSA. Walk through it in [how to get your MC authority](/resources/how-to-get-mc-authority).
- **BOC-3** (process agent designation). Details in our [BOC-3 filing guide](/resources/boc-3-filing).
- **Insurance filed with FMCSA** by your insurer. See [owner-operator insurance](/resources/owner-operator-insurance-cost).
- **UCR** (Unified Carrier Registration), IRP plates, IFTA, and heavy vehicle use tax (Form 2290). Which of these apply depends on your truck, weight, and routes, so confirm each one. For fuel tax, read [IFTA reporting explained](/resources/ifta-reporting-for-owner-operators).

## What do I need for drug and alcohol testing?

Drivers with a CDL are subject to DOT drug and alcohol testing. Owner-operators join a random testing consortium and register in the FMCSA Clearinghouse. Full details are in our [drug and alcohol consortium guide](/resources/drug-and-alcohol-consortium-owner-operators).

## What do I need for hours of service?

Most interstate drivers who must keep records of duty status use an ELD, unless they qualify for an exemption. See [ELD requirements for owner-operators](/resources/eld-requirements-owner-operators).

## What records does a new carrier have to keep?

Expect to keep organized records in a few areas:

- **Driver qualification file:** application, license, medical certificate, and driving record checks. As an owner-operator you are also the driver, so yours counts.
- **Hours-of-service records** from your ELD or logs
- **Vehicle inspection, repair, and maintenance records**
- **Drug and alcohol testing records**
- **Accident records and insurance documents**

## What is the New Entrant safety audit?

New carriers enter FMCSA's New Entrant Safety Assurance Program, which monitors a new carrier's safety performance for 18 months. FMCSA says a safety audit takes place within the first 12 months of operation. The audit checks that your safety management basics are in place: drug and alcohol testing, driver qualification files, hours-of-service compliance, and vehicle maintenance records.

## What do new carriers commonly get wrong?

Problems tend to cluster around the same gaps: no active drug and alcohol testing program, an incomplete driver qualification file, missing inspection and maintenance records, and registration details that don't match across FMCSA, insurance, and the bank. Setting these up before your first load is far easier than fixing them during an audit.
